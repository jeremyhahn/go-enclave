# Plan: fuzz the residue scanner's maps parser and maps walk (#366)

## Objective

`internal/memscan` parses /proc/self/maps lines (`ParseMapsHeader`) and walks
them (`find`, with `mappingContains`, `readLen`, `sweptMapping`,
`kernelMapping`). Neither has a fuzz target. This row adds two:

- `FuzzParseMapsHeader`: never panics; when it accepts a line, lo <= hi and
  both equal the hexadecimal fields of the line; it accepts every well-formed
  header whose bounds fit a uintptr.
- `FuzzFind`: synthetic maps text over a `bytes.Reader` image at a fixed base,
  with arbitrary ranges, permissions and paths. It never panics, never reads
  outside a range it sweeps (the ReaderAt is wrapped to assert it), and
  reports a line exactly when its swept range holds the needle. Free-form maps
  text is walked as well, with reads held to the ranges its lines list.

## Slices, in order

1. Test commit: `memscan_fuzz_test.go` (portable, `FuzzParseMapsHeader`) and
   `memscan_fuzz_linux_test.go` (`FuzzFind`, needs the linux-only walk), with
   seeds from real-shaped lines: heap, stack, vdso, vvar, anonymous, labeled
   anonymous, memfd, deleted files, huge addresses, malformed lines. A crash
   the fuzzer finds becomes a seed skipped with `t.Skip("defect: ...")`.
2. One fix commit per defect the fuzzers expose, dropping its skip.
3. Changelog fragment `changelog.d/366-memscan-fuzz.md`; a simplify pass over
   the new test code.

## Out of scope

- The signal-frame residue test (its own open PR).
- Any change to which mappings the sweep covers.
